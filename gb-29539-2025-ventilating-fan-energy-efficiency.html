GB 29539-2025: China's New Fan Energy Efficiency Standard, Explained

BAER (Zhejiang Baer Electrical Technology Co.,ltd) — factory-direct exhaust and inline duct fans — explains what China's mandatory 2026 efficiency standard covers, what it excludes, and what it means for buyers.

From 1 November 2026, household ventilating fans sold in China must meet three energy grades and new standby limits. Here is the plain-language reading for importers — including why this domestic standard is different from the certificates an export shipment needs.

Last updated: November 24, 2026

China updated its mandatory energy efficiency rules for ventilating fans in late 2025, and the new version took effect on 1 November 2026. If you source fans from China — for domestic sale there or for re-export — the standard number GB 29539-2025 is likely to appear in a supplier email or a tender document. This guide explains what it actually says, which products fall inside its scope, and how to read a claim without confusing a domestic efficiency grade with an export certificate.

We write this as standard interpretation, not as a product claim. BAER does not state a GB energy grade for its fans, and this page explains why that distinction matters for an export buyer.

The short version

GB 29539-2025 is a mandatory Chinese national standard titled "Minimum allowable values of energy efficiency and energy efficiency grades for household and similar range hoods and ventilating fans". Published 31 October 2025 and effective 1 November 2026, it merges and replaces GB 29539-2013 (range hoods) and GB 32049-2015 (household AC ventilating fans). For fans it sets three grades (grade 1 best), makes grade 3 the market-entry floor, adds standby ≤2.0 W / off-mode ≤1.0 W, and brings DC fans into scope for the first time.

It is a domestic market-access rule for China. It is not an import requirement for Saudi Arabia, the UAE, the EU or North America, and it is not a substitute for CE, CB, SASO/SABER or an IEC motor class. Buyers exporting from China should understand it because it shapes what a compliant Chinese factory builds — but the certificate your shipment needs is set by the destination country.

What changed, and when

The 2025 edition is a consolidation. Two older standards were folded into one, the grade scale was cut from five to three for range hoods, and the fan section was rewritten around airflow-per-watt with new standby and off-mode limits.

The three energy grades

For ventilating fans the standard grades products by efficiency value — airflow per unit of input power, expressed as m³/(min·W) and measured at defined static-pressure points. Grade 1 is the most efficient; grade 3 is the minimum allowed to be sold. Different fan types (A, B, D and non-duct ceiling types) have their own tables, and the metric differs by impeller size or rated airflow.

GradeMeaningStatus under the standard
Grade 1Highest efficiency bandAbove the mandatory floor; a marketing advantage in China
Grade 2Middle bandCompliant
Grade 3Lowest allowed bandEnergy efficiency limit value — the market-entry floor
Below grade 3Does not meet the limitCannot be produced or sold in the Chinese domestic market

Two details matter in practice. First, the grade is measured at a stated duty point (for B and D types, at 25 Pa static pressure) — a free-air airflow number alone cannot establish a grade. Second, the standard sets a standby limit of 2.0 W and an off-mode limit of 1.0 W across covered products, a requirement the previous fan standard did not contain. A fan with an always-on electronic controller now has to account for that draw.

Reading the value correctly takes care. The unit m³/(min·W) means cubic metres of air moved per minute for each watt of input power: a higher number is better. A-grade tables are indexed by impeller diameter, while B and D types are indexed by rated airflow and measured at 25 Pa. Because the two families use different denominators, a grade 1 B-type fan and a grade 1 A-type fan are not claiming the same numerical efficiency — each is meeting the threshold for its own category. Compare within a type, and always at the stated test point.

DC fans and the new standby rule

The biggest technical change in the 2025 edition is the inclusion of DC ventilating fans. The earlier GB 32049-2015 covered household AC fans only, so a buyer comparing an older "GB compliant" claim with a new one may be looking at two different scopes. If a supplier quotes GB 29539-2025 for a DC model, that is now a meaningful statement; quoting the older standard for the same model is not.

The 2025 edition also introduces a standby power limit of 2.0 W and an off-mode limit of 1.0 W. These are measured separately from the running efficiency under the standard's test method. In practical terms, a fan with an always-powered receiver, Wi-Fi module or electronic speed controller has to keep that standby draw low enough to pass. A model that performs well when running can still fail the standard because of what it consumes when switched off — a detail that rarely appears in a sales brochure.

For a buyer, the standby figure is worth asking about on any fan with electronics. Two fans with the same running efficiency can differ in annual consumption if one idles at 0.5 W and the other at 1.9 W, and the difference grows with hours of connected but idle time.

Which products are in scope

The standard is written for household and similar-use products, not for industrial equipment. A fan falls inside it only when it meets all three electrical and size conditions, and is not caught by one of the exclusions.

ConditionLimit in GB 29539-2025
SupplySingle-phase, rated voltage ≤250 V
Input powerRated ventilation input ≤500 W
Impeller diameter≤500 mm
DriveMotor-driven ventilating fans and multi-function units with ventilation
Motor typeAC and, new in 2025, DC fans

Fan types A, B, C and D follow the definitions in GB/T 14806-2017. The standard sets separate efficiency tables for A-type, non-duct ceiling A-type, B-type, B-type multi-function units and D-type fans. C-type fans, bidirectional-discharge fans, and B or D types with maximum static pressure below 25 Pa are outside the standard.

What this means for export buyers

For a buyer importing Chinese fans into Saudi Arabia, the Gulf or Europe, GB 29539-2025 is background context, not your clearance document. The standard is a Chinese domestic energy-efficiency requirement. Your shipment is cleared against the rules of the destination country: in Saudi that means the G-Mark and SABER route; in the EU, CE and applicable ecodesign rules; in the Gulf states, their own conformity schemes.

So when a supplier says "our fans meet GB 29539", ask two questions: does the standard actually cover this model (many industrial fans do not), and which document are they really offering — a domestic China energy-label filing, or a test report relevant to your market? A grade 1 label for the Chinese market does not clear a container in Jeddah.

The confusion is understandable, because "efficiency" appears in several unrelated frameworks. The table below separates them. Each exists for a different reason and a different authority; none automatically satisfies another.

How to check an efficiency claim

Energy-efficiency claims follow the same verification discipline as any other technical document: get the report, match it to the model, and confirm it belongs to the right market.

  1. Name the exact model and type. Ask whether the fan is A, B, C or D type, its voltage, input power and impeller diameter — the first two decide if the standard even applies.
  2. Ask for the filing, not just the letter. In China, energy-label products are registered on the China Energy Label system; a model-specific filing number should be checkable.
  3. Check the grade and the number. Grade 3 is the floor. Ask for the measured efficiency value and the static-pressure point, not just "grade 1".
  4. Check standby and off-mode figures. Under the 2025 edition these are part of compliance, so a missing value is a gap.
  5. Keep it separate from export certificates. File the energy-label document beside — not instead of — the CB report, G-Mark or SABER records your destination requires.

For the wider set of documents and how to check them, see the exhaust fan certifications guide, the manufacturer verification guide and the SABER/SASO guide. For the motor-side framework that applies in many export markets, read the IE efficiency classes guide.

Frequently asked questions

What is GB 29539-2025?

A mandatory Chinese national standard for the energy efficiency of household and similar range hoods and ventilating fans. It was published on 31 October 2025, took effect on 1 November 2026, and replaces GB 29539-2013 and GB 32049-2015.

Does it apply to export fans?

It is a Chinese domestic market-access rule. A fan exported to Saudi Arabia, the UAE or Europe is cleared against that destination's requirements, not against GB 29539. Understanding the standard helps you read a Chinese supplier's documents, but it does not replace CE, CB, G-Mark or SABER.

How many energy grades are there?

Three, with grade 1 the most efficient. Grade 3 is the energy efficiency limit value — the lowest grade allowed to be produced and sold in the Chinese domestic market. Products below it cannot be sold there.

When did the standard take effect?

1 November 2026. Products manufactured or imported before that date receive a 13-month transition, extending to 1 December 2027.

Are DC fans covered?

Yes. The 2025 edition brings DC ventilating fans into scope for the first time, alongside the AC fans covered by the earlier standard.

Which fans are excluded?

Fans designed specifically for industrial use, commercial cooking extraction, fans for corrosive or explosive atmospheres, fans for refrigeration or air conditioning, embedded and marine fans, and C-type or low-static-pressure B/D types are outside the standard.

Does a grade 1 energy label prove a fan is efficient in my application?

Not by itself. The grade is measured at defined test points in the Chinese system. For your installation, compare airflow and input power at your actual duty point, and check the performance curve.
