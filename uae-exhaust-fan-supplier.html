Certification & Documentation

Two layers apply to electrical goods entering the UAE. The first is the Gulf G-Mark — the GSO conformity mark that covers household fans across all GCC states. The second is the national ECAS scheme, now administered by the Ministry of Industry and Advanced Technology (MoIAT), which absorbed the former ESMA. Regulated electrical appliances need it before they can be imported or sold.

RequirementWhat it means for your order
G-Mark (GSO)Gulf-wide conformity mark for household fans, based on the GSO adoption of IEC 60335-1 and IEC 60335-2-80, registered through the GSO GCTS platform.

Test evidenceISO/IEC 17025 reports mapped to the applicable UAE.S standard. A CB report is accepted as a basis, but UAE national differences — Type G plug, 230V/50Hz, RoHS — must be added.
RoHSRestricted-substance testing against the UAE.S RoHS standard covering lead, cadmium, mercury and the other listed substances.

The two most common causes of delay are an incomplete technical file and a test report that does not match the exact model and electrical configuration being imported.

UAE Grid Reality: 230V, 50Hz, Type G

The UAE supply is 230V / 50Hz — not the 220V many older specification sheets still quote, and critically not the 60Hz used across the border in Saudi Arabia. Running a 50Hz motor on 60Hz turns it roughly 20% faster than designed, which changes airflow, current draw and motor life. If you buy for both markets, treat them as two separate specifications and say so on the inquiry.

Plug configuration is equally fixed: UAE sockets are BS 1363 (Type G) three-pin rectangular. Fans are supplied with a Type G plug or a permanently fixed lead; loose travel adaptors are not accepted on regulated appliances. Where a unit is hard-wired into a building management system, confirm the isolator and earthing arrangement with your MEP contractor before you order rather than on site.

Heat, Salt and Stack Effect: What the UAE Does to a Fan

Three conditions shape UAE specifications, and only the first is obvious.

  • Ambient heat. Summer peaks of 45–50°C are routine, and a ceiling void behind a sun-facing façade runs hotter still. Specify motors with a high-temperature insulation class and check the rated ambient on the datasheet — a fan rated Ta 40°C is operating outside its design envelope.
  • Coastal corrosion. Dubai, Abu Dhabi and Sharjah sit in salt-laden air with relative humidity reaching 90% in summer. Standard painted-steel housings and grilles pit within a couple of seasons. For coastal and podium-level installations, specify corrosion-protected finishes and stainless fasteners.
  • Stack effect in towers. In high-rise buildings the pressure difference between inside and outside of the shaft pushes air through a fan even when it is switched off, and resists it when running. Every fan on a tower exhaust riser needs a working backdraft damper, and must be sized against the static pressure of the riser — not its free-air rating. This is the single most common reason a correctly sized fan under-performs in a tower.

Fine dust carried on shamal winds loads grilles and impellers between cleaning cycles. Choose models with removable, washable grilles and confirm the maintenance access before the ceiling is closed.

Shipping and Logistics to the UAE

Sea freight: 15–22 days from Ningbo or Shanghai to Jebel Ali — the Gulf's largest container port and the default entry point for most UAE orders — or Khalifa Port for Abu Dhabi consignments. Both handle full-container and LCL.

Air freight: 3–5 days to Dubai International or Al Maktoum for samples and urgent top-up orders; economic only below roughly 300 kg.

Terms: FOB Ningbo/Shanghai as standard, with CIF, CFR and DDP available on request. Every unit is individually cartoned with foam protection, palletised, and photographed before loading.

Clearance: UAE customs is among the fastest in the region — typically one to two days once the ECAS certificate is registered against the importer's licence. Arriving without it is what delays cargo, not the port.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are your fans suitable for centralised ducted systems in towers?

Yes. Our inline mixed-flow duct fans are designed for duct pressure and can be installed remotely in ceiling voids, keeping occupied spaces quiet — the standard approach for UAE high-rise residential.

What noise levels can you achieve?

Our silent bathroom fan range runs 25–35 dB(A) depending on model. Ask for third-party noise test data for the specific model you are specifying.

Can you do private label for our UAE brand?

Yes — logo printing on the grille or housing, custom packaging, user manuals and barcode service are all available at MOQ from 500 pcs per model.

Who holds the ECAS certificate — you or us?

Neither, strictly: it must be held by a UAE-registered company with a valid trade licence, which in practice is your importer or authorised agent. A Chinese factory cannot hold it directly. What we do is supply the complete technical file and accredited test reports so your representative can file without a technical query loop.

Do tower installations need anything special?

Two things. Fit a backdraft damper on every unit connected to a shared riser — otherwise stack effect drives air backwards through idle fans and you get odour transfer between floors. And size the fan against the static pressure of the riser rather than its free-air rating; our inline mixed-flow range is built to hold airflow under that pressure.
