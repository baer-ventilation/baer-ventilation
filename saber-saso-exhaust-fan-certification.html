SABER & SASO Certification for Exhaust Fans: The Saudi Import Process

Last updated: September 8, 2026

What importers need before a ventilating fan can clear Saudi customs — and the documents that cause most rejections.

Saudi Arabia does not accept a CE mark on its own. Every regulated consignment must be registered on SABER, the electronic conformity platform run under the SALEEM programme of SASO (Saudi Standards, Metrology and Quality Organization). Without a valid shipment certificate, the goods are held at port.

This guide is written for importers, contractors and procurement teams buying exhaust and ventilating fans from China. It explains the sequence, the two certificates, the HS classification and the technical file — plus the mistakes that most often delay clearance.

The Short Version

For fans, the sequence is: G-Mark first, then SABER.

Fans sit in the GCC-regulated appliance category. A G-Mark (GCC) certificate is the entry ticket; the Saudi importer then uses it to register the model on SABER and obtain a PCoC, and a SCoC is issued for each shipment. A G-Mark by itself allows circulation in the other GCC states — it is not sufficient for Saudi Arabia.

SASO, SALEEM and SABER: What Each One Does

These three names are often used interchangeably, which is where most confusion starts:

The single most expensive mistake: a SCoC cannot be issued retroactively. If the container lands before the SCoC is approved, the shipment sits at port accruing demurrage. Build this step into your production schedule, not into your shipping schedule.

The Process, Step by Step

  1. Confirm the G-Mark. Fans are GCC-regulated, so a valid G-Mark (GCC) certificate covering the model is required first. It is normally valid for three years and the unit must carry the GCTS traceability marking.
  2. Classify the product. The importer registers on SABER with the HS code and product category. Getting the HS code wrong is the most common cause of rejection.
  3. Choose a conformity assessment body (CAB). A SASO-approved CAB — SGS, Intertek, TÜV, Tabseer and others — reviews the file. The importer selects one from the SABER dashboard.
  4. Submit the technical file. CB test report and certificate, G-Mark, EMC reports, product photos, ratings label artwork and factory documentation.
  5. PCoC issued. Once the CAB is satisfied, the product certificate is issued electronically and linked to the importer's profile.
  6. Apply for the SCoC per shipment. Upload the commercial invoice, packing list and bill of lading. Saudi customs then validates the SCoC through FASAH to release the goods.

Typical processing is 5–15 working days depending on whether additional testing or a factory audit is triggered. Regulated products with a complete file move faster than non-regulated ones with gaps.

HS Codes for Exhaust Fans

Classification drives both the duty rate and which technical regulation applies. For fans the dividing line is motor output:

ProductHS headingBasis
Wall, window, ceiling and panel fans ≤125 W8414.51Self-contained motor, output not exceeding 125 W
Duct / inline and higher-power exhaust fans8414.59Other fans — above 125 W or not in the listed types
Blades, motors, housings8414.90Parts

Saudi uses a 12-digit national HS code, so the six-digit heading is only the starting point. Confirm the full code with your importer or CAB before filing — a mismatch between the invoice and the SABER record is a standard rejection trigger.

Technical Standards and Test Reports

For the safety assessment, fans are evaluated against:

  • IEC 60335-1 — general safety requirements for household and similar electrical appliances.
  • IEC 60335-2-80 — the particular requirements specific to fans.
  • CB scheme report and certificate — normally used as the evidence base. For Saudi these must cover the 60 Hz national deviation (Saudi runs 60 Hz, while much of the world's test data is generated at 50 Hz).
  • EMC — typically CISPR 14-1 / 14-2 and harmonic limits under IEC 61000-3-2. Saudi has been screening EMC documentation more strictly in recent cycles, so do not treat it as optional.

Relevant test points for ventilating fans include motor locked-rotor temperature rise, blade guard protection, resistance to heat and fire, and — for bathroom units — the degree of protection against moisture (IP rating).

Check expiry dates before you ship. CB reports are only accepted within their validity window. An expired report is one of the most common causes of a stalled PCoC application, and renewing it mid-shipment costs far more than checking a date.

Why Applications Get Rejected

  • Wrong or incomplete HS code. Confirm the 12-digit national code, not just the 6-digit heading.
  • Expired CB or test reports. Check validity dates at the quotation stage.
  • Applying for the SCoC after arrival. It cannot be backdated.
  • Invoice data not matching the SABER record. Model names, quantities and importer details must be consistent across every document.
  • Missing Arabic labelling. Ratings plate and packaging artwork need to satisfy Saudi labelling requirements.
  • Assuming G-Mark alone is enough. Saudi requires SABER registration on top.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do exhaust fans need SABER certification for Saudi Arabia?

Yes. Fans are a GCC-regulated category. In practice you need a G-Mark (GCC) certificate first, then the importer registers the model on SABER for a PCoC, and a SCoC is issued per shipment before arrival.

What is the difference between PCoC and SCoC?

The PCoC approves the model and is valid for one year, tied to a specific Saudi importer. The SCoC is issued per shipment for customs clearance and must be obtained before the goods arrive — it cannot be issued retroactively.

Which HS code applies to exhaust fans?

Fans with a self-contained motor of 125 W or less generally fall under HS 8414.51; larger or duct-mounted units above 125 W are typically 8414.59. Saudi uses a 12-digit code, so confirm the full classification with your importer or CAB before filing.

Which safety standards apply to fans?

IEC 60335-1 (general requirements) together with IEC 60335-2-80 (particular requirements for fans). A CB test report and certificate are normally used as evidence, and for Saudi they must cover the 60 Hz national deviation.

Is a G-Mark enough to sell in Saudi Arabia?

No. G-Mark covers the wider GCC market, but Saudi Arabia requires SABER registration on top of it. A G-Mark alone allows circulation in the other GCC states, not in Saudi.

Who is responsible for obtaining the certificates?

The Saudi importer registers on SABER and applies for both certificates, since they are tied to the importer's commercial registration. The manufacturer supplies the supporting technical file — CB report, G-Mark, EMC reports, photos, label artwork and ISO documentation.
