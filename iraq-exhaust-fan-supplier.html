Certification & Documentation

Iraq runs a pre-shipment conformity programme — ICIGI — administered by the Central Organization for Standardization and Quality Control (COSQC) under Law No. 54. Electrical appliances sit inside the controlled list, so a Certificate of Conformity (CoC) is required before the goods can clear.

RequirementWhat it means for your order
Pre-shipment CoCIssued by a COSQC-authorised body — SGS, Bureau Veritas, TÜV Rheinland and others — after document review and physical inspection of the shipment.
Destination re-inspectionIraq re-checks at the port of arrival: certificate authenticity, cargo-to-document consistency and cargo condition, with release issued only after that check. This step has no equivalent in most Gulf programmes — a CoC alone does not guarantee release.
Southern ports are stricterControls at Umm Qasr and the southern gateway have tightened since 2025: categories that need only a COI further north may still require a full CoC in the south. Confirm against the actual discharge port before you book, not the country.

Inspection fees are banded by FOB value — approximately USD 320 for shipments up to USD 80,000 FOB, with a further review charge per container at destination. Testing is additional, and materially cheaper when you already hold a current accredited report: a valid test report under a year old typically cuts both cost and file-review time.

Iraq's Grid: Why Wide-Voltage Motors Are Not Optional

Iraq's public supply is nominally 230V / 50Hz, but the practical reality is wide fluctuation and frequent generator changeover. Mains voltage in many districts swings well outside the tolerance European stock is built for, and during changeover to private or district generators the supply dips hard before it recovers.

That is why we recommend wide-voltage motors — specified across a 180–250V operating band rather than a single nominal voltage — combined with thermal overload protection for Iraqi orders. A fan that merely tolerates 230V will not survive a summer of brownouts; a fan designed for the band will.

Note the labelling trap: the CoC file accepts range marking, but a single 60Hz marking is rejected. If you are buying for Iraq and Saudi in the same season, those are two different nameplates.

50°C Summers and Fine Dust: Specifying for Iraq

  • Extreme ambient heat. Summer temperatures above 50°C are common in the south and in Baghdad. Specify a high insulation class and check the rated ambient — standard Ta 40°C units run hot and lose bearing life quickly.
  • Fine airborne dust. Iraq's dust season loads impellers and clogs grilles much faster than the coastal Gulf states. Sealed bearings matter more here than anywhere else in the region, and grilles should be removable for washing without dismantling the housing.
  • A regional humidity split. Basra and the southern marshlands sit at high humidity for much of the year while Baghdad and the north are dry. Southern coastal projects need the same corrosion-protected finish as an Arabian Gulf order; inland projects do not.
  • Continuous duty. In much of Iraq exhaust fans run continuously through the cooling season rather than intermittently. Size for continuous duty and confirm the bearing specification accordingly.

Shipping and Logistics to Iraq

Iraqi routing has more variables than any other market we serve, and the right choice depends on where the cargo actually has to land.

  • Sea to Umm Qasr — the default for southern and central Iraq: roughly 22–30 days from Ningbo/Shanghai including transhipment, plus inland haulage to Baghdad.
  • Land bridge via Turkey — Mersin or Iskenderun, then overland through the Zakho crossing for Erbil, Mosul and the north. Faster to the north than routing through the south.
  • Aqaba and overland — an alternative for central Iraq when Gulf sailings are congested.
  • Air freight — 4–7 days to Baghdad, Erbil or Basra for samples and urgent orders.

Tell us the final delivery city rather than just the country and we will quote the routing that actually lands it there. Terms are FOB Ningbo/Shanghai as standard, with CIF and DDP available on request.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can your motors handle voltage fluctuation?

Yes. We offer wide-voltage motor versions with an operating range of approximately 180–250V. This is one of the most requested options for Iraq and is strongly recommended for long motor life.

What is the minimum order for a first trial?

MOQ is 100 pcs per model, and mixed models can be combined into one container. Many Iraqi buyers start with a mix of window and ceiling models to test the market.

Do you provide Arabic packaging?

Yes. Carton artwork, labels and installation manuals in Arabic are available, typically from 500 pcs per model.

Is a CoC enough, or is there a check on arrival?

There is a check on arrival, and it is the step most first-time exporters miss. Iraqi authorities re-verify the CoC's authenticity, compare the cargo against the documents and inspect condition before issuing release. We prepare the file so it matches the physical shipment exactly — including carton markings and model codes — which is what makes that check pass.

Why do you recommend wide-voltage motors for Iraq?

Because the nominal 230V is not what the fan will actually see. Mains voltage swings widely and generator changeovers dip hard. We specify motors across a 180–250V band with thermal overload protection, which is the difference between a fan that survives the cooling season and one that does not.
