Power Supply: Why Wide-Voltage Models Matter

Yemen's nominal supply is 220 V / 50 Hz, but in practice generator-backed operation and an unstable grid mean the voltage a fan actually sees can swing widely — sustained readings of 180–250 V are common, and generator changeover produces short spikes well above that.

  • Ask for wide-voltage motors (typically 180–250 V rated) rather than standard 220 V ±10%. The cost premium is small and it removes the most common warranty claim.
  • Insist on thermal overload protection. A fan stalled by low voltage will sit there drawing current and cooking its windings unless the thermal cut-out trips.
  • Avoid sensitive electronics where the grid is poor. EC motors are efficient but less tolerant of spikes. On generator-heavy sites, either stay with a robust AC motor or specify surge protection on the circuit.
  • Check the plug and cord specification against local practice at the time of order — this varies by project and getting it wrong means rewiring on site.

Dust and Maintenance Reality

Inland Yemeni sites accumulate dust fast. A fan specified on clean-air performance will lose airflow steadily as the impeller and grille load up. Two practical measures that cost almost nothing:

  • Specify a removable, washable intake filter or screen on fresh-air and supply applications, and write the cleaning interval into the maintenance schedule (quarterly is typical for inland sites).
  • Oversize the duty point by 10–15% to allow for performance fall-off between cleaning cycles. This is the same duct-loss margin we recommend in our CFM calculator, applied to fouling instead.

Sealed-for-life bearings are the right choice for most Yemeni projects — they cannot be re-greased, but they also cannot be forgotten, and there is rarely a maintenance technician with a grease gun on site.

Import Documentation and Clearance

Documentation problems delay more Yemeni shipments than freight problems do. Plan for the following and confirm the current requirement with your clearing agent before we ship, since rules change:

  • Commercial invoice and packing list — we issue these; confirm whether your bank or authority requires them attested.
  • Certificate of Origin — issued by the China Council for the Promotion of International Trade (CCPIT). Required for most shipments; ask early as it adds a few days.
  • CE declaration and test reports — supplied for our models. Not a Yemeni legal requirement in itself, but frequently requested by clearing agents and end clients as evidence of electrical safety.
  • Bill of lading — to order, unless your buyer has specified otherwise.

We supply the full document set as standard and will flag anything unusual. What we cannot control is the attestation chain (chamber of commerce, and where required, consular legalisation) — that runs through your agent in-country, and it is worth confirming the current requirement before you fix a delivery date.
