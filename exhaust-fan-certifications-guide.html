Exhaust Fan Certification Guide: CE, CCC, CB, SAA and ETL

By BAER Ventilation  ·  August 2026  ·  8 min read
Home › Exhaust Fan Certification Guide

Certification documents are part of a complete exhaust fan procurement file. They help buyers, contractors and distributors compare the quoted model with the documentation required for a project and destination market.

However, a logo on a catalogue is not the same as model-specific evidence. Before approving a purchase order, match the certificate, test report, nameplate, datasheet, sample and shipment documents.

Important: Certification requirements can vary by product configuration, destination, installation and project authority. Treat this page as a buyer's document-checking guide, then confirm the current requirement with your importer, conformity body or local authority.

1. The Five Certification Names Buyers Commonly See

CE

Ask which product family and technical documentation the CE declaration covers. Match the model, voltage, frequency and applicable standards stated in the supplier's file.

CCC

Ask whether the quoted product or configuration falls within the relevant Chinese certification scope. Request the document that covers the exact model family.

CB

Ask for the CB certificate or report reference applicable to the fan model. Check that the electrical configuration and covered variants match your order.

SAA

For projects connected with Australia or markets requesting SAA documentation, confirm the applicable product scope and document holder before ordering.

ETL

For North American projects that request ETL evidence, verify the listing or report information against the exact product, model and installation requirements.

3. The Document Pack to Request Before Ordering

1

Product datasheet

Request airflow, static pressure where relevant, noise, input power, dimensions, installation method, IP information and operating conditions.

2

Certificate or declaration

Request the applicable certificate or declaration and the model list or scope showing which product family is covered.

3

Test report

Ask for the test report associated with the product and check the tested configuration, electrical data and issuing laboratory or body.

4

Nameplate and sample photos

Compare the motor nameplate, label, markings and sample photos with the approved quotation and documents.

5

Shipment document plan

Confirm which documents will be included with the commercial shipment and which files will be sent electronically for the project record.

4. Questions for Your Supplier

  • Which exact models and variants are covered by this document?
  • Does the document cover the voltage and frequency required for my destination?
  • Does it cover the supplied motor, grille, shutter and housing configuration?
  • Who is the legal holder of the certificate or declaration?
  • Is the document current for the production version being quoted?
  • Can the document be shared with my importer, inspector or project consultant?
  • What happens if the approved sample and mass-production model differ?
  • Which additional documents are needed for my destination market or project?

Frequently Asked Questions

Should an exhaust fan certificate show the exact model?

The buyer should check that the document identifies the applicable product family or exact model and matches the voltage, frequency, motor and configuration in the quotation.

Is a certificate alone enough for an exhaust fan purchase?

No. Check it together with the model datasheet, test report, nameplate, sample and destination-market documentation.

Which certification documents should I request from a supplier?

Request the applicable certificate, test report, covered model list, issuing-body information, declaration or conformity document where applicable, and the document version used for your shipment.

How to Verify a Certificate Is Real

Any supplier can share a certificate. The real question is whether it matches the fan you are actually buying. Verify four things before you approve an order:

  • Issuing body: a recognised lab or notified body (TÜV, SGS, Intertek and similar) — not an in-house "certificate" printed by the factory.
  • Covered model list: the certificate should name the model family or model number, matching your quotation exactly.
  • Validity: check the expiry date — many certificates lapse.
  • Traceability: a certificate number you can look up on the issuing body's public database is worth far more than a PDF.

Certification Costs and Timelines (Typical)

Certification is a real cost line in ventilation sourcing. As a rough guide:

  • CE (LVD + EMC): a few weeks and a modest fee for a standard model — usually already absorbed into the product cost by an established manufacturer.
  • CB scheme report: weeks, and reusable across multiple national certifications — the smartest single investment if you plan to sell in several markets.
  • ETL / cETL: a more involved and costly program, justified for the North American market.
  • Retesting a variant: adding a voltage or motor option can require a partial retest, which is why you should confirm the certification covers your exact configuration.

Working with a manufacturer who already holds the certificates for your target market avoids these costs entirely — you inherit the certification instead of funding it.

The Three Documents That Travel With Every Order

When your container ships, three documents should arrive with the electronic file pack:

  • The product datasheet — model, voltage, frequency, airflow, power, dimensions and weight.
  • The certificate and declaration — the market-specific conformity documents (CE DoC, SABER PCoC, and so on).
  • The test report — the lab results behind the certificate, including the standards and test conditions.

If any of the three is missing or references a different model, stop and ask before you accept the shipment. A mismatched document is one of the most common causes of customs hold-ups in the Middle East.

Checklist Before You Pay the Deposit

  • The certificate names your exact model and voltage/frequency.
  • The issuing body is a recognised lab or notified body, not the factory itself.
  • The certificate is within its validity period.
  • The supplier has shipped to your destination country before.
  • You have the test report, not just the certificate.
