Exhaust Fan MOQ: What It Is and How to Work With It

Last updated: September 3, 2026

Why exhaust fan factories have minimum order quantities, and how to get the best terms.

MOQ — minimum order quantity — is the smallest number of units a factory will produce or ship in a single order. For exhaust fans, a low MOQ is one of the first things importers look for, but it is also one of the most misunderstood. This guide explains why MOQs exist, what is normal in the ventilation industry, and how to work with them.

Why Factories Have an MOQ

A factory does not simply "have" an MOQ as a policy — it is the result of real production economics:

  • Setup cost: changing a production line between models takes time, and that time has a cost that must be spread across the batch.
  • Material purchasing: motors, wire, plastic resin and packaging are bought in minimum lots. A small order still consumes a full material lot.
  • Quality control: testing and inspection effort per batch is similar whether you order 50 or 500 units.

This is why a factory quoting an unrealistically low MOQ is often a signal that it is not really the factory.

What Is a Normal MOQ for Exhaust Fans?

Order typeTypical MOQNotes
Stock / standard model100 pcs per modelFastest lead time
OEM / customised500 pcs per modelIncludes branding and configuration changes
New private mouldHigher, mould-dependentTooling cost amortised across the order

BAER's standard MOQ is 100 pcs per model for stock models and 500 pcs for OEM/custom, with mixed models accepted in one container.

How to Work With an MOQ

  • Start with a sample: a single-unit sample (freight collect) lets you test before committing to the MOQ.
  • Mix models in one container: if you need variety rather than volume, combine several models to reach the total.
  • Choose stock models first: they carry the lowest MOQ and the shortest lead time.
  • Negotiate on long-term volume: a factory will often lower the MOQ for a buyer who commits to a repeat schedule.

What Raises the MOQ

  • Custom voltage or frequency (e.g. 60Hz or 110V) — a dedicated motor batch.
  • Private-label packaging and branding — printing and artwork setup.
  • A new mould or colour — tooling cost to amortise.
  • Unusual specifications — anything off the standard configuration.

The more standard the model, the lower the MOQ and the faster the delivery.
