What Actually Determines the FOB Price of an Exhaust Fan

Last updated: September 10, 2026

Why two factories quote 40% apart for the same fan — and how to compare the quotes fairly.

Two Chinese factories quote for "the same" 12-inch panel exhaust fan and the prices differ by 40%. One of them is not lying — they are quoting for different products. This guide breaks down what actually goes into an FOB price, which specifications move it most, and how to compare two quotes so that you are comparing the same thing.

Why Two Quotes Differ So Much

An exhaust fan is a simple product with a short bill of materials, which means small specification differences produce large price differences. The four most common sources of a gap:

  • Motor specification. Copper versus aluminium windings, bearing type, and whether the motor is rated for the destination frequency.
  • Material grade. Panel steel thickness, whether the panel is metal or engineering plastic, and the grade of that plastic.
  • What is left out. A shutter, a cable, a plug, a grille, an earth lead — each is small, and a quote that omits several of them is cheaper for a reason.
  • Who is quoting. A trading company buying from a factory and a factory selling direct do not have the same cost base. See our guide on how to verify a manufacturer.

Why the Unit Price Falls With Quantity

A factory's cost per unit is not constant. Some costs are incurred once per order regardless of size:

  • Setting up and changing over the production line
  • Buying materials in minimum lots — motors, wire, resin and packaging are all bought in batches
  • Preparing artwork, printing plates and carton tooling for a private-label run
  • Inspection and documentation effort, which is similar whether the batch is 200 units or 2,000
  • Shipping space — a part-full container costs almost as much to book as a full one

Spread those fixed costs over more units and the unit price falls. This is the honest reason factories have an MOQ, and it is covered in more depth in our MOQ guide.

How to Compare Two Quotes Fairly

Put the two quotes side by side and check every line. If a field is blank in one quote, it is not "included" — ask.

  • Motor power, and winding material
  • Voltage and frequency rating
  • Airflow and the condition it is measured at (free air, or working point)
  • Noise in dB
  • Shutter type
  • Bearing type
  • Cable length and plug standard, or hard-wired
  • Packing: pcs per carton, carton dimensions, gross weight, CBM
  • FOB port
  • Lead time after deposit
  • Payment terms
  • Certification documents included, and which certificates
  • Warranty period, and what it covers

Two quotes only become comparable when all thirteen lines are filled in on both.

Why the Cheapest Quote Usually Costs the Most

The saving on a fan is small and the cost of failure is large. A failed motor inside a finished building means a return visit, a replacement unit shipped at your expense, a damaged client relationship and possibly a rejected batch. The price difference between an aluminium and a copper winding is a fraction of the cost of one site callback.

Where a quote is significantly below the market, look for one of four things: a lower-grade winding, a sleeve bearing instead of a ball bearing, a missing shutter or cable, or a spec sheet that describes a different product from the one that will ship.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does an exhaust fan cost from China?

It depends almost entirely on motor specification, panel material and quantity. For a reference point, we publish the 6-inch KLT-10B at USD 4.58 to 6.58 per piece FOB Ningbo at MOQ 100 pcs. Larger models and OEM configurations are quoted per order.

Why do exhaust fan quotes vary so much between suppliers?

Usually because the products differ, not because one supplier is overcharging. Copper versus aluminium windings, ball versus sleeve bearings, whether a shutter and cable are included, and whether the seller is a factory or a trading company all move the price. Compare quotes line by line.

Does the price go down if I order more?

Yes. Part of a factory's cost is incurred once per order regardless of size — line setup, minimum material lots, artwork and carton tooling, inspection and shipping space. Spreading those fixed costs over more units lowers the unit price.

Is a copper winding worth the extra cost?

For any installation where the fan runs continuously or sits in high ambient temperatures, yes. A copper winding has lower electrical losses, so the motor runs cooler and lasts longer. Aluminium is acceptable for light intermittent duty.

Why do you not publish prices for all your models?

Because the specification genuinely changes the price. Voltage, frequency rating, panel colour, shutter configuration and quantity all move the number, so a single published figure would be misleading for most enquiries. We quote per order instead, usually within one working day.
