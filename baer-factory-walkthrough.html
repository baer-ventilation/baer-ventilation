1. Product Development and Model Definition

A reliable production process starts before assembly. The buyer and supplier should define the model, application and performance target clearly.

1

Application and installation

Define whether the fan is for a bathroom, kitchen, window, ceiling, wall, duct, warehouse or other installation. Confirm dimensions and installation constraints.

2

Performance target

Confirm airflow, static pressure where relevant, noise, power input, voltage and frequency. For Middle East projects, include ambient-temperature and dust or moisture requirements.

3

Configuration approval

Record the motor, blades, housing, grille, shutter, wiring, packaging and label configuration in the quotation and approved sample file.

2. Components and Incoming Inspection

Fan performance depends on the consistency of motors, bearings, capacitors, blades, housings, grilles and electrical parts. Buyers should ask how incoming parts are checked before entering assembly.

3. Assembly and In-Process Checks

Assembly is where individual components become a repeatable product. A buyer visiting the factory or arranging a video tour should look for clear work instructions and checkpoints rather than only the number of workers on a line.

  • Work instructions identify the model and configuration being assembled.
  • Wiring, terminals, fasteners and protective parts are checked before closure.
  • Rotating parts are fitted and checked for rubbing, imbalance or abnormal noise.
  • Labels, nameplates and accessories match the approved order.
  • Nonconforming products are separated and recorded instead of being mixed into finished stock.

4. Electrical, Airflow and Noise Testing

Testing should reflect the product's intended use. Ask the supplier which checks are performed on every unit, which are performed by batch and which are verified by a third party or independent inspector.

Test areaWhat it tells the buyer
Electrical safety and inputWhether the product matches the required voltage, frequency, current and power configuration.
Start-up and running testWhether the motor starts correctly and operates without abnormal heat, noise or vibration.
Airflow and pressureWhether the fan performance is suitable for the application and duct resistance.
Noise and vibrationWhether the model fits residential, commercial or industrial project expectations.
Final visual inspectionWhether the finish, label, accessories and packing match the approved sample.

Do not accept “tested” as a complete answer. Ask what was tested, under which conditions, with which instrument and against which specification.

5. Final Inspection and Export Packing

Export packing is part of product quality. A fan that leaves the line in good condition can still arrive damaged if carton protection, accessories, palletization or container loading are not controlled.

  1. Confirm the final quantity and model mix against the packing list.
  2. Check labels, manuals, accessories and spare parts.
  3. Inspect cartons, internal protection, pallet or container requirements and shipping marks.
  4. Photograph the finished order before loading when the contract requires shipment evidence.
  5. Keep the final inspection result with the approved sample and purchase order.

6. What Buyers Should Prepare for a Factory Audit

Whether you visit in person, use a video tour or appoint an inspection company, prepare a model-specific checklist.

  • Approved model number and technical datasheet
  • Voltage, frequency, power and target performance
  • Required certificates and destination-market documents
  • Sample photos and approved packaging
  • Quantity, ship date and inspection window
  • Warranty, spare-parts and corrective-action expectations

7. BAER Factory Information for an RFQ

To help BAER prepare a useful response, include your destination country, application, quantity, installation type, voltage, frequency, airflow or pressure target, certification requirement and packaging or label needs.
