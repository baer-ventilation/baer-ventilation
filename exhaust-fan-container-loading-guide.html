Container Loading: How Many Exhaust Fans Fit in a 20GP or 40HQ

Last updated: September 10, 2026

Per-model loading figures, the calculation behind them, and why fans are volume-limited, not weight-limited.

Container planning is where a good quotation turns into a bad landed cost. Loading fewer fans than the container can hold means paying freight on empty space; over-declaring the load means a re-stuffed container at the port and a delay. This guide gives the per-model loading figures for the KLT series and shows the calculation so you can check it against your own freight forwarder's numbers.

*Usable volume assumes 85% loading efficiency, which is realistic for regular export cartons. Palletised cargo loads lower; hand-stacked cartons can reach 90%. Payload figures vary by carrier and by the road weight limits of the destination country.

Exhaust fans are almost always volume-limited, not weight-limited. The tables below confirm it: every KLT model fills the container before it reaches the weight limit.

How the Calculation Works

Cartons = usable volume (m³) ÷ carton volume (m³)
Pieces = cartons × pieces per carton
Gross weight = cartons × gross weight per carton

Round the carton count down — you cannot ship part of a carton. The carton volume and weight figures below are the same ones on our KLT series specification table.

Worked Example: A Mixed Container

Mixed-size containers are accepted, and mixing is usually the right commercial answer — a single-model container is rarely what a distributor actually needs. Take a 20GP with two models:

  • Usable volume: 33.2 m³ × 85% = 28.22 m³
  • KLT-10B: 100 cartons × 0.110 m³ = 11.00 m³ → 2,000 pcs
  • Volume remaining: 28.22 − 11.00 = 17.22 m³
  • KLT-20B: 17.22 ÷ 0.145 = 118 cartons → 708 pcs
  • Total: 2,708 pcs in 218 cartons, 28.11 m³, about 3,805 kg gross

Note what happened to the weight: 3,805 kg against a 28,200 kg payload. Volume ran out long before weight did — the same pattern as every single-model row in the table above.

Practical Notes for the Packing List

  • Carton count is what your forwarder needs. Pieces are for your sales side; cartons, CBM and gross weight are for the freight calculation.
  • Stacking height. Export cartons for panel fans are designed to stack, but the top layer still needs protection. Confirm the stacking limit with us if your warehouse will store them high.
  • Pallets reduce the load. If your customer requires palletised delivery, expect to lose 10 to 15% of the usable volume. Tell us at order stage so the packing plan reflects it.
  • Destination road limits. Some countries cap road vehicle gross weight below the container's rated payload. For heavy loads, check the inland leg, not just the ocean leg.
  • Ask for the loading plan. On a mixed order we will calculate the optimum combination for your target quantities and send the loading plan with the quotation.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many exhaust fans fit in a 20ft container?

It depends on the model. On our published packing data, a 20GP takes about 5,120 of the 6-inch KLT-10B, about 2,130 of the 8-inch KLT-12B, about 1,160 of the 12-inch KLT-20B, and about 258 of the 18-inch KLT-30B. Mixing sizes is accepted.

How many exhaust fans fit in a 40HQ container?

Roughly double the 20GP figure for the same model — about 11,800 of the 6-inch KLT-10B, about 4,900 of the 8-inch KLT-12B, about 2,680 of the 12-inch KLT-20B and about 595 of the 18-inch KLT-30B, based on our standard export cartons.

Are exhaust fans limited by weight or volume in a container?

Almost always by volume. On our packing data, every KLT model fills the container well before it approaches the weight limit — a 20GP of 6-inch fans comes to about 5,120 kg against a payload of roughly 28,200 kg.

Can I mix different fan sizes in one container?

Yes. Mixed-size containers are accepted and are usually the better commercial answer for a distributor. Send us your target quantities by model and we will calculate the loading plan and quote it as one shipment.

How much volume do I lose if I palletise the load?

Expect to lose 10 to 15 percent of the usable volume compared with hand-stacked cartons. If your customer requires palletised delivery, tell us at order stage so the packing plan and the loading figures are calculated on that basis.
