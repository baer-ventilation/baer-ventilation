Panel Exhaust Fan Installation Guide: Cut-Out, Height & Wiring

Last updated: September 10, 2026

Cut-out sizes, mounting position, wiring practice, sealing and the commissioning checks that catch a failed install.

A panel exhaust fan is a two-hour job for someone who has done it before and a two-day job for someone who has not. This guide covers the five decisions that determine whether the installation works: where to cut, how high to mount, how to wire it, how to seal it, and what to check before you sign it off.

Electrical work must be carried out by a qualified electrician and must comply with the wiring regulations of the country of installation. The guidance below is for planning and specification purposes. It is not a substitute for local code or for a licensed installer.

Before You Cut Anything

  • Wall construction. Panel fans need a solid fixing. In a cavity or stud wall, add blocking or a mounting frame behind the panel before the cut-out — after the hole is open it is much harder.
  • What is in the wall. Check for cables, conduit, water and gas services. In a kitchen wall, check for the hob extract route. Never cut blind.
  • Where the exhaust goes. A fan that discharges into a closed roof void or a lightwell moves air from one enclosed space to another. Confirm the discharge is to outside air.
  • Make-up air. There must be a path for replacement air to enter — a door undercut, a louvre or a transfer grille. Sealed rooms starve the fan.
  • Supply. Confirm the voltage and frequency on site before ordering. See our 60Hz guide — a 50Hz fan on a 60Hz supply is a real and common problem in Saudi Arabia.

Cut the opening 3–5 mm larger than the stated figure so the fan body drops in without forcing. Check the squareness of the cut with a diagonal measurement — an out-of-square hole shows as a visible gap at one corner of the panel.

Step 2: Mounting Height and Position

Position matters more than height. The fan should pull air across the room, not short-circuit from the nearest opening to the nearest exit.

  • General rule: mount high on the wall, diagonally opposite the main air entry (usually the door). This forces the air to travel the length of the room.
  • Bathroom: high on the wall, opposite the door, above or beside the shower rather than directly over it. Directly over a shower pulls steam before it rises and leaves the rest of the room damp.
  • Kitchen: close to the hob but not directly above it, and not in the splash zone of a cooker hood. A panel fan is not a cooker hood — for heavy grease, specify a hood and duct it properly.
  • Workshop: high level at the end of the building, with inlet louvres at the opposite end so the air sweeps the working area.
  • Do not: mount immediately next to a window or door that is usually open, and do not face an open flue or gas appliance — you can pull combustion products back into the room.

Step 3: Wiring

  • Supply: the KLT series is 220 V / 50 Hz single phase as standard. 110 V / 60 Hz and other voltages are available on OEM orders.
  • Isolation: provide a local means of isolation — a fused spur or an isolator within reach of the fan, not only a breaker in a distant panel. Anyone servicing the fan needs to be able to prove it is dead.
  • Cable: three-core (live, neutral, earth). The fan must be earthed. Cable size depends on the circuit protection and run length — your electrician will size it to local regulation.
  • Protection: for a bathroom or any wet area, follow the local zoning rules for what may be installed inside each zone, and confirm the fan's IP rating is suitable for the position.
  • Controls: a simple on/off switch is standard. Where a run-on timer or humidity sensor is required, specify it at order stage — it is a factory option on the KLT series, not a field modification.
  • Direction of rotation: check it before final fixing. Reversed rotation gives airflow in the wrong direction and is a common cause of a "faulty" fan on first commissioning.

Step 4: Sealing and Weatherproofing

  • Seal the perimeter. Fill the gap between the fan body and the wall with a suitable sealant or mortar. An unsealed gap lets air bypass the fan entirely and defeats the installation.
  • Protect the outside face. Where the discharge is exposed to wind-driven rain, fit an external weather louvre or hood. The fan's own auto-shutter stops dust and back-draft; it is not a rain seal.
  • Coastal and desert sites. Salt air and wind-driven sand attack hinge pins and bearings. Specify the external hood and expect a shorter cleaning interval — see our notes on Middle East climate.
  • Duct connection. Push the duct fully onto the spigot and secure it. A duct that has slipped off inside a ceiling void is invisible and destroys the performance.

Step 5: Commissioning Checks

  • Fan starts and runs without tripping the circuit protection
  • Airflow direction is outward — confirm with a tissue or a smoke pencil at the grille
  • The auto-shutter opens fully when running and closes when stopped
  • No rattling or scraping; the impeller is clear of the grille
  • Air can be felt entering through the door undercut or transfer grille
  • No draught or whistling at the wall perimeter

Maintenance

  • Every 3 months (dusty or coastal sites, monthly): remove the grille, clean the impeller and the shutter hinge pins.
  • Every 6 months: check the fixings and the sealant perimeter; re-torque if the wall has moved.
  • Annually: check the cable gland and the terminal connections for heat discolouration.
  • Always isolate the supply before any of the above.

Frequently Asked Questions

What size hole do I need for a panel exhaust fan?

Use the fan's install size, not its panel size. For the KLT series the wall cut-out ranges from 160 by 160 mm on the 6-inch KLT-10B to 480 by 480 mm on the 18-inch KLT-30B. Cut 3 to 5 mm oversize so the body drops in without forcing.

How high should an exhaust fan be mounted?

High on the wall, diagonally opposite the main air entry so air sweeps across the room. In a bathroom, mount opposite the door rather than directly over the shower, and avoid mounting right beside a window or door that is normally open.

Can I install a panel exhaust fan myself?

The cutting, fixing and sealing are within reach of a competent DIY installer, but the electrical connection must be made by a qualified electrician and must comply with local wiring regulations. The fan must be earthed and needs a local means of isolation.

Does an exhaust fan need its own circuit?

It needs a means of local isolation such as a fused spur, and it should be protected to suit the cable and the installation position. Whether it shares a circuit or has a dedicated one depends on local regulation and on whether the fan has a timer or sensor.

Why is my exhaust fan not extracting properly after installation?

The four usual causes are a stuck auto-shutter, a duct that has come off its spigot, a room with no path for make-up air to enter, and reversed rotation. Check those four before assuming the fan itself is faulty.
