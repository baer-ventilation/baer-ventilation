Inline Duct Fans: The Complete Guide

Last updated: September 3, 2026

How inline duct fans work, how to size one, and where each type performs best — factory-direct from BAER.

An inline duct fan is a fan mounted inside a duct run, so air enters and leaves along a straight line. Unlike a wall or window fan, it can be hidden in a ceiling void or along a duct, and — in its mixed-flow form — it holds its airflow against the pressure of long ducts, bends and grilles. This guide explains the types, how to size one, and where each works best.

Types of Inline Duct Fans

For most residential and commercial ducted ventilation, a mixed-flow inline fan is the sweet spot — it moves air quietly while holding airflow through a typical duct run. See the full comparison in our inline duct fan vs axial fan guide.

How to Size an Inline Duct Fan

Sizing has three steps:

  • Calculate required airflow from room volume and air changes per hour — use the CFM calculator.
  • Add margin for resistance: every bend, grille and metre of duct reduces real-world airflow. Add 10–20%.
  • Match the diameter: the fan diameter should equal the duct diameter. Undersized ducts create pressure and noise.

Applications

  • Bathroom extraction through a ceiling duct — where a wall fan cannot reach the outside.
  • Kitchen hood / extraction — grease-laden air through a duct run.
  • Long ducted systems in villas, hotels and offices — centralised extraction.
  • Bedrooms and living spaces — where the fan must be heard as little as possible.

Installation Tips

  • Mount with the arrow pointing in the airflow direction.
  • Use rigid duct where possible — flexible duct adds resistance and noise.
  • Support the fan to avoid vibration transfer to the structure.
  • Leave access for cleaning and maintenance.
