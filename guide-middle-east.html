Exhaust Fan Buyer's Guide for Middle East Markets

Voltage, IP rating, airflow sizing and certifications — what to check before you order from a Chinese factory.

Sourcing exhaust fans for Saudi Arabia, UAE, Iraq, Kuwait or Qatar involves specific technical requirements that differ from European or North American markets. This guide covers what experienced importers check before placing an order.

1. Voltage & Frequency Requirements

The Middle East predominantly uses 220–240V / 50Hz. This is the same as Europe, but always confirm with your local distributor. Key points:

  • Saudi Arabia, UAE, Kuwait, Qatar: 220–240V / 50Hz ✓
  • Some older buildings in Iraq may have 110V circuits — verify with the end client
  • Always specify voltage when requesting samples — running a 220V motor on 110V will damage it

2. IP Rating for Hot & Dusty Environments

Middle Eastern climates — high temperatures, sand, and humidity in coastal areas — demand higher ingress protection ratings:

ApplicationRecommended IPWhy
Indoor residential / officeIP44Standard dust & splash protection
Bathroom / kitchenIP44–IP55Moisture resistance required
Industrial / warehouseIP55–IP65Dust ingress common in desert regions
Outdoor / rooftop installationIP65+Full dust-tight + water jet protection

3. Airflow Sizing (CFM / m³/h)

Correct airflow is critical for effective ventilation. A common rule of thumb:

  • Residential bathroom (5–10 m²): 50–100 m³/h
  • Kitchen (10–20 m²): 150–300 m³/h
  • Commercial space (50–200 m²): 600–2,000 m³/h
  • Industrial / warehouse: Calculate ACH (air changes per hour) × room volume

Middle East tip: In hot climates, increase the calculated airflow by 20–30% to compensate for higher ambient temperatures reducing fan efficiency.

5. Common Mistakes When Sourcing from China

  • Not requesting a sample first — always test 1–5 units before mass order
  • Ignoring noise level specs — Middle East residential buyers are sensitive to noise; ask for dB(A) rating
  • Choosing price over bearing type — ball bearings last 30,000+ hours vs. sleeve bearings at 10,000 hours
  • Not checking motor thermal protection — essential in high-ambient-temperature environments (45°C+)
  • Skipping factory audit — request a video tour or third-party inspection report
